For the 30th Anniversary documentary — 30th Anniversary Script.html. Built from the thirteen sit-down interviews transcribed so far (Don Matheny, Pete & Kristi Sciacchetano, Rob Carter, Johnny Hill, Dan Lord, Wayne & Classy Francis, Emy Vazquez, Josh & Jessica Fuentes, Marco & Tiina Geraci, and, new this week, Adam Bartlett, Mundo Meneses, Parker VanBlaricom and Adrian & Leigh Wright — about 11 hours of film) plus the six 30 Stories content assessments, which hold thirty more voices, one per year.

What changed since 17 September. Four interviews landed exactly where the first draft said the film was thinnest, 2002–2017, and one of them hands the film its ending. Adam Bartlett dates the building story (Germantown Parkway bought around 2007, the Collierville YMCA, Houston Levee opened February 2010, the auditorium begun in 2016, the Global Hub breaking ground in August 2026). Mundo Meneses carries Outreach (the first Serve Truck bought from the stage, Read to Lead, the COVID food lines). Parker VanBlaricom is the Axis kid of 2005 who has run the Axis Conference since 2018. The Wrights bring South Africa and the Bible School — the two things the script’s Future section asks for clips of.

The short version. Go chronological; what’s on film now covers all thirty years. Its shape is a rhyme the script does not yet use — the 2020s are the 1990s again — and, new this week, a spine that runs the whole length of the film. After Nairobi, John and Leslie “felt like the Lord told them that he would replace them 200 times over on the mission field” (MM 076). Thirty years later the church that sent them is joining the family: “Nairobi Lighthouse Church that sent Pastor John and Pastor Leslie to Memphis… They’re becoming a part of the Life Church family” (MM 120–121). The story can open in Nairobi and close in Nairobi. Five movements, in order, each owning one or two values. The bookends stay: the Intro, John’s VO over a scene shot for it (John and Leslie entering the building, the green room, the team, the morning’s service flows), and the Future sit-down. So the film starts in the present and the story goes back to Nairobi from there. The “Features” block shrinks to a short Identity bridge (the pressure test’s reshape, below).

Two cautions. The middle is filled — by staff. Movement III now has six voices, and every one of them worked for the church; the member’s-eye view of 2002–2017 comes only from the 30 Stories witnesses, so pin them. And the founding is still over-served — Matheny, the Sciacchetanos and Rob Carter all cover 1996–2002 well; hold Movements I–II to about a third of the film.

The proposal — five movements

The script’s Section II is already chronological. What changes is that each stretch of time gets an owner-value, the six “Features” move inside the stretch where the film demonstrates them, and the last movement is cut to rhyme with the first two — and now to close the circle the first one opens.

Intro · John’s voiceover over a morning at the church · ~2 min, as scripted

The script’s opening stands, and it now has its picture. Pastor John’s voiceover (“Did I see all of this when we were just starting out 30 years ago?”) plays over a scene shot for it: John and Leslie entering the building and going into the green room, members of the team meeting them there, and the two of them looking over the service flows for the morning. At the script’s 1:30 mark the montage of worship and preaching takes over and lands on the card The Life Church: 30 Years of Faithful Ministry.

  • Why it suits the chronological shape: the film opens in the present, inside the “all of this” John is asked about: the building, the team, a morning’s services. Movement I then goes back to Nairobi, and the rest of the film answers his question in order. The Future sit-down brings it back to now.
  • Shoot note: keep rolling past the service flows, through the script’s “praying before we walk into service” and the walk out. The intro may not need it, but it gives the edit an option for the last shot: after the Future sit-down, John and Leslie walking into the service, closing the frame the intro opens. The script’s production timeline pairs this b-roll with the John & Leslie sit-down.

I · Sent — Nairobi to Memphis, 1993–1996 · owns Missions and Vision · ~15%

Born on a mission field, in three voices that never coordinated: Matheny in his living room, Rob Carter calling it “bizarre,” Kristi holding Leslie’s letters from Kenya. Anchor scene: the living-room call to plant (DM 037), the retreat and the stadium send-off (DM 044, 117), the phone call (DM 064 or the clean retake 119), Don’s connection to Dr. King (066). New: the word that sets up the whole film — “he would replace them 200 times over on the mission field” (MM 076). Mundo tells it second-hand; it must be John’s line on 8 October, planted here and paid off in Movement V.

  • Values shown at the origin: Diversity — forty tribes (DM 032–034). Creativity — Leslie’s calligraphy (DM 014). Worship and Preaching — John on guitar, teaching the Word (DM 015).
  • First story beat after the title card (candidate): Pete, 1976 — “we have been friends ever since, 50 years later” (PK 002) — then Don.

II · Planted — seven people and a rented room, 1996–2002 · owns Community and Generosity · ~20%

Launch Sunday, the crash to half, six years of church in a box, the ask (“we could really use you in Memphis,” PK 018), the grandmother’s verdict (PK 019), John on guitar at the Home Builders (RC 002), the grocery store (RC 011–017). Rob’s “we’re not reaching far enough” (019) is the thesis line of the film and lands here, the first time the church over-reached on purpose. Parker’s family walking in during an altar call in August 2000 (PV 008–013) is the member’s-eye picture of these years.

  • Values shown: Community — “just a small group of people coming together” (PK 023). Generosity — “you can’t out give God” (RC 016). Diversity — “people from all walks of life, all races” (RC 017).
  • 30 Stories witnesses pinned: Lisa (1996), Steve (1998), Jane (1999), the VanBlaricoms (2000), the Sullivans (2002).
  • Script beats absorbed: The Launch · The Early Years (Dino — missing; drop “Pastor John able to quit his job” — he never had one; Nairobi supported them, DM 047, 057) · Breakthrough 2000 (fast and billboard — John’s) · Germantown Parkway 2002.

III · Multiplied — the city and the next generation, 2002–2017 · owns Outreach, Young People, Worship · ~25%

No longer the thin middle — now the fullest movement, and the risk flips to crowding. Katrina is the doorway: three future leaders arrive through one storm — Dan handed the relief with no chainsaw (DL 013), Johnny hearing John on the radio (JH 002–003), Parker meeting Dan on the Mississippi coast (PV 030–033). Then four threads, each with one owner:

  • Young People (Dan → Parker): thirty kids to a sold-out conference. Dan founds the culture (the chant, DL 090; “I want a next gen revolution,” DL 079); Parker is the proof it worked — the 7th grader of 2005 handed the keys at Starbucks in 2018 (PV 047–049), 200 students in 2009 to over 2,000 by 2022 (PV 103–105). Kaymen (30S 2014) is the member’s version.
  • Outreach (Mundo): “not only have church, but be the church” (MM 002) — red bags to Read to Lead (MM 013–017, 031–033, 036–038), and the scene: John and Leslie standing up one Sunday to buy the first Serve Truck, $75,000 by the end of the weekend (MM 052, 074–075).
  • Buildings (Adam): the whole multi-site gap is closed. Owning Germantown Parkway (AB 019) → John’s prayer and phone call — “we need to get out of this location” (AB 028) → the YMCA, baptisms in the pool (AB 065–072) → the Forest Hill trade (AB 074–078) → Houston Levee, Feb 2010 (AB 079–080) → the auditorium, “a dream come true, but it’s not the dream” (AB 116–117). His “I’m getting emotional” over selling Germantown Parkway (AB 053) is the movement’s quiet moment.
  • Worship (Johnny): Kids Life stage boxes to overseeing every location (JH 006–019); “We create space for God to create moments” (JH 038).
  • The TV years (Brian, next week): the weekly program from 2008 is the script’s “influence in the city” beat, which no one else covers.
  • Santiago (25 May 2014) closes the movement as the bridge to a family of churches (JJ 008–019) — and Mundo’s team met the Fuenteses on a mission trip (MM 089), so the Missions thread from Movement I runs straight into it.

The pressure test’s proof-point is now a fair test. Rough-assemble this movement first: if six voices play as scenes, the structure holds; if they play as a staff roll-call, cut to three threads (Young People, Outreach, Buildings) and let Worship live in the Identity bridge.

  • Script beats absorbed: Growth 2002–2008 · Going Multi-Site · Axis Conference · Serve Trucks · Santiago · Houston Levee · (Zoe — Leslie’s, still missing).

IV · Family — pastors to pastors, 2017–2021 · owns Diversity and Leadership · ~15%

Two couples who ran their own churches asked John and Leslie to be their pastors before anything merged — Wayne’s email (WC 029–031), Emy’s dream (EV 004) — and the value the film keeps naming is covering, not expansion: “they don’t micromanage. They’re not territorial” (EV 098). Diversity’s most articulate voice is Wayne (Black Fist White Knuckles, WC 074–078, and the podcast it birthed, Leadership in Black and White, WC 079–090). COVID ends the movement, and now has its Memphis side: the church moves online in a week and John hosts daily livestream updates so that no one is left uncared for (Brian, next week); the drive-through food lines (MM 022–030); Santiago the weekend it shut (WC 062–066); Easter from an office in New York (WC 070).

  • Repetition risk: Dan, Emy and Wayne all met John at ARC. Tell one (Wayne’s) and montage the others.
  • Script beats absorbed: New York Merge · COVID (pictures, online content, growth).

V · Just Getting Started — the founding story, again, 2020–2027 · owns Vision · ~25%

Cut to rhyme: Florence’s ninety people in an unheated lemon house against the Home Builders hall; Fitchburg’s eviction-then-building and Santiago’s storm-then-Ritz against the Mega Market; Johannesburg’s sixteen people and 1,000 rand (AL 040) against Memphis’s seven.   • Values shown, second time around: Vision — “a church in Europe for the kids of our kids” (MT 130); Emy’s “30 locations in 30 years”; Adrian’s intercontinental hub and ten churches in Africa (AL 207–212). Faith in crisis — Emy’s cancer, Marco’s COVID false start. Young People — Axis in Fitchburg, six to 185 (EV 049). Community — “We don’t feel alone anymore” (MT 114). Outreach — the township community center in Johannesburg (AL 220–226; MM 094–095).
  • Script beats absorbed: Crazy Expansion · The Unfolding Picture of Locations (montage) · Future (new building, new Bible School).

What this changes in the script, concretely

  • Shrink §IV Features to an Identity bridge (3–4 min), don’t delete it. The values are shown inside the movements; the bridge states them once, plainly — and Outreach earns a slot there now that Mundo is on film. Keep the framing question as John’s VO into it.
  • Move §III The Unfolding Picture of Locations into that bridge, so every campus — domestic, international, Nashville, DC, the prisons — is named and seen, and Movement V’s montage carries only the story locations.
  • Retire §V Interviews as a block, but pin ten witnesses: Lisa (1996), Steve (1998), Jane (1999), the Sullivans (2002), the VanBlaricoms or Meneses (2005), Dwayne (2010), Kaymen (2014), Bella (2019), Yunna (2020), Travis Yu (2024). They are the only member voices in Movement III.
  • Plant and pay off the Nairobi word. John tells “200 times over” in Movement I; the film ends on Nairobi Lighthouse joining the family. That is the strongest ending on film, and it is already true.
  • Keep the bookends as written: the Intro VO, now over the scene shot for it (entering the building, the green room, the team, the morning’s service flows; see Intro above), and the Future sit-down.

Pressure test — five lenses, one verdict (17 September; updated)

A blind panel (advocate, skeptic, realist, audience, best-alternative) tested the first draft:

  • Strongest point in favour: the material is already chronological and the 30 Stories clips are cut one per year, so pinning them reduces work; the origin/echo rhyme gives the last act an argument.
  • Strongest objection: coverage clumped at both ends, the longest movement had the least film, and deleting Features removed the one place the values are said out loud.
  • The audience risk: a member at a campus with no interview checks one thing — do I exist in this story — which is why the roll-call moves into the bridge and the Nashville/DC/prison pickups are planned.
  • The realist’s clock: rough cut 30 September, one editor who is also shooting.

Verdict: RESHAPE — keep the five movements and the rhyme (as kinship: we are them, never they are behind us), shrink Features to a bridge that names every campus, pin ten witnesses, and direct the John & Leslie sit-down into the movements’ values. Update, 25 September: the objection that killed the middle is answered — Movement III has six voices — so the cheapest test is now also the fair one: rough-assemble Movement III first. A same-day check before that: grid the five movements × values and mark which cells already have a logged clip.
